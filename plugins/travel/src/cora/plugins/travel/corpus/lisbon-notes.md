# Lisbon — notes from a week in March

## Getting in from the airport

The red metro line runs from the airport to Alameda, where you change for the green line
into Baixa. It is the cheapest way in and takes about twenty-five minutes. Buy a
rechargeable Navegante card at the machine rather than a single ticket — the card itself
costs a little and every journey after that is cheaper.

## Neighbourhoods

Alfama is the old quarter below the castle: stairs, tiled façades, and the 28 tram
grinding through it. Beautiful, and loud until late.

Baixa is flat, central and rebuilt on a grid after the 1755 earthquake. Convenient, and
mostly restaurants for visitors.

Príncipe Real sits above Bairro Alto, quieter, with the botanical garden and the better
coffee. Twenty minutes downhill to the centre, and a hard climb back.

## Miradouros

The viewpoints are the point of the city. Senhora do Monte is the highest and the
emptiest at sunrise. Santa Catarina fills up at sunset and has a kiosk. São Pedro de
Alcântara is reachable by the Glória funicular from Restauradores if the hill has beaten
you.

## Eating

Lunch is the cheap meal: a *prato do dia* in a tasca runs well under a tourist dinner.
Cervejarias serve seafood late. Pastéis de nata are everywhere and the ones at Manteigaria
are worth the queue — they are made in front of you and come out hot.

## Day trips

Sintra is forty minutes by train from Rossio. Go early: the palaces cap their entries and
the queues by eleven are long. Cascais is a slower, flatter alternative along the coast
from Cais do Sodré, thirty-five minutes, with a beach at the end of it.

## What caught me out

Almost nothing opens before nine. Many museums close on Mondays. The hills are steeper
than any map suggests, and the tiled pavement is slippery in rain.
